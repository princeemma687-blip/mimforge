## A. Physics Paper 3 structure

1. Is Paper 3 worth **50 marks** with a duration of **2¾ hours**?
2. Are there **3 questions, of which candidates answer any 2**?
3. Is each question split **(a) 21 marks** for the experiment (readings →
   table → graph → slope/intercept → deduction → precautions) and
   **(b) 4 marks** of linked theory? Where exactly do the other marks sit
   if this split is wrong?
4. Is it true that Question 1 is typically mechanics, Question 2 heat or
   optics, Question 3 electricity — and how reliable is that pattern in
   recent years?
5. How many **precautions** must a candidate state for full credit in part
   (a) — is it two?

## B. The simple pendulum question form

6. Is the standard provided apparatus: pendulum bob, metre rule,
   stopwatch, retort stand with clamp, thread, split cork? Anything else
   candidates are routinely given?
7. Are the lengths **L = 90, 80, 70, 60, 50 cm** the standard set? Have
   recent papers used a different range or step?
8. Is the number of oscillations timed **20**? Has WAEC used 10 or 20 in
   recent papers?
9. Is **L measured from the pivot (point of suspension) to the centre of
   the bob**? How is this worded in the question, and how is it marked if
   a candidate measures to the top of the bob instead?
10. Which graph forms has WAEC actually set in recent years — **T² against
    L** (our demo), T against √L (2018), log T against log L (2022)? Which
    is currently most likely?
11. When the deduction is g, what is the **accepted value/range** in the
    marking scheme (e.g. 9.8 or 10 m/s², or a band around the supervisor's
    value)? Within what **tolerance** does a candidate's g earn full,
    partial, or no accuracy credit?
12. Is the slope expected from a **large triangle** on the graph, and what
    fraction of the graph grid must the plot fill (we assume ≥ half, with
    1/2/5 scales)?

## C. Table, graph and marking mechanics

13. Table format: must columns be headed **quantity/unit**, values to a
    **consistent number of decimal places**? For L in cm, is **one decimal
    place** (90.0) the expected form? For t and T²?
14. Are **both t₁ and t₂** (repeat timings) expected in the table, with the
    mean used — or is a single timing accepted?
15. Do method marks survive a wrong final value of g (correct working,
    units, averaging shown)? How is the accuracy mark tapered?
16. Are observations and deductions ever required to be recorded
    separately in Physics (as observation vs inference is in Chemistry),
    or is that distinction Chemistry/Biology only?
17. Chief Examiner reports list the main mark-losers as: scale-reading
    errors, inconsistent decimal places, poor slope determination, wrong
    graph scales, missing/wrong units, poor precautions. Is that still the
    current list, and what would you add?

## D. Precautions — exact creditworthy wording

18. Are these two precautions credited as written?
    (1) *"The bob was displaced through a small angle and released without
    a push; conical oscillation was avoided (oscillation kept in one
    vertical plane)."*
    (2) *"Timing was started and stopped as the bob crossed the fiducial
    mark at the equilibrium position; the support was firm / the
    apparatus was shielded from draught."*
19. Must precautions be stated in the **past tense**, naming a specific
    error source **and** the action taken? What phrasings are commonly
    rejected (e.g. "avoid error", "avoid parallax error" without saying
    how)?
20. Is "parallax error avoided when reading the metre rule" and "zero
    error of the stopwatch/metre rule checked" credited for this
    experiment?

## E. Timing method and the machine's difference

21. Do examiners expect timing to start/stop at the **equilibrium
    (fiducial) mark** rather than at maximum displacement? Why — and is
    explaining that reasoning creditworthy in part (b)?
22. Our teaching machine times oscillations with an **optical gate at the
    equilibrium position**, not a stopwatch. Is it pedagogically honest —
    and acceptable to state in the lesson — that the machine's timing
    method differs from the candidate's stopwatch method while measuring
    the same quantity? What wording would you use with students?
